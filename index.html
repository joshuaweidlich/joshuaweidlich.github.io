The challenge of studying a technology’s learning benefits

Media comparison studies of GenAI in education

About the talk

Since ChatGPT arrived, studies comparing learning with and without AI have multiplied, and so have confident claims about its benefits. Yet a widely cited meta-analysis on the topic has since been retracted. Audits of the underlying studies also show that most comparisons cannot say what actually produced the effects they report.

None of this is new: film, television, computers and the internet were each met with the same kind of media comparison study, new medium versus old, and the results were just as inconclusive. The talk explains why such comparisons rarely answer the question they ask, and why generative AI makes the problem harder. It then proposes four questions that turn a comparison into a meaningful claim, and illustrates them with two examples, one from my own research on feedback.

Four questions

Four questions to ask of any study that compares learning with and without a technology.

  1. What exactly was the intervention? Tool and model version, prompts and instructions, time, teacher support, and what students actually did.
  2. Compared to what? What the comparison group did in the same time, with the same content and support.
  3. What was learned, and how was it measured? Knowledge or skill; tested with or without AI access; independent test, researcher-made test, or self-report.
  4. Which claim does the study support? A programme effect, a design principle, a mechanism, or a description. A claim about the medium as such is usually out of reach.

The handout adds further checks for reading or planning a study, each with a source.
