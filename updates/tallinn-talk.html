The challenge of studying a technology’s learning benefits

Talk · 11 November 2026 · Tallinn University
Open research seminar of the AI Lab for Research on Teaching and Learning

Media comparison studies of generative AI in education: why they rarely answer the question they ask, and four questions that help judge what a study can show.

Slides (PDF)Handout (PDF)

About the talk

Since tools like ChatGPT arrived, many studies comparing learning with and without AI have emerged, some with confident claims about its benefits. This year, a widely cited meta-analysis on the topic has since been retracted. Audits of the underlying studies also show that most comparisons cannot say what actually produced the effects they report.

Film, television, computers and the internet were each met with similar kinds of media comparison studies. The talk explains why such comparisons rarely answer the question they ask, and why generative AI makes the problem harder. It then proposes four questions that turn a comparison into a meaningful claim, and illustrates them with two examples, one from my own research on feedback.

Four questions for any study

  1. What exactly was the intervention? Tool and model version, prompts and instructions, time, teacher support, and what students actually did.
  2. Compared to what? What the comparison group did in the same time, with the same content and support.
  3. What was learned, and how was it measured? Knowledge or skill; tested with or without AI access; independent test, researcher-made test, or self-report.
  4. Which claim does the study support? A programme effect, a design principle, a mechanism, or a description. A claim about the medium as such is usually out of reach.

The handout adds further checks for reading or planning a study, each with a source, and a short reading list.
